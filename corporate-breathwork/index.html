EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Breathwork for Companies

"Regulated people think better, lead better and work better together. Everything else follows from that."

When the nervous system is chronically activated, no amount of strategy, training, or motivation fixes the underlying problem. Breathwork for Corporate works at the level where the change actually happens.

THE MECHANISM IS PHYSIOLOGICAL

Your nervous system does not know the difference between a tiger and a Monday full of back to back calls. Breathwork gives it a way out of that state in minutes, not weeks of trying to think your way calm.

THE APPROACH IS SAFE FOR EVERYONE

No one is asked to share, perform, or explain what they’re carrying. Grounded in Polyvagal Theory, every session is built so a first timer and someone with years of practice both leave regulated, not exposed.

THE RESULTS ARE TRANSFERABLE

You are not learning to feel good for an hour in a room with candles. You are learning a switch you can use before a hard conversation, a pitch, or a decision that matters, on your own, with no facilitator needed.

What happens during a corporate breathwork session?

Every session is designed to bring the team to a clear, specific place: calmer under pressure, more resilient through a demanding stretch, sharper and more focused before a decision that matters.

The process starts with slowing the breath and guiding attention inward, working with the body’s own signals instead of bypassing them. It often involves curated music or vibrational instruments that guide participants into a deep, receptive state, where the shift begins to happen physically, in the breath, the chest, the shoulders, and moves outward from there: a clearer head before a hard decision, more patience in a tense conversation.

These sessions work well for:

  • Team offsites and building days
  • EOFY wrap-ups
  • Leadership retreats
  • Sales kickoffs
  • Onboarding new teams
  • Pre-launch or high-pressure project periods
  • Annual conferences and summits

questions

What I got asked the most

Is this spiritual or "woo"?

No. I explain everything through physiology: what happens in the nervous system when we're under stress, and how the breath can change that response in a few minutes. You don't need any belief or previous experience to take part, just curiosity and a body that breathes.

Do people have to share anything personal?

No, never. Everyone works with their own breath, at their own pace. Nobody is asked to talk about what they're going through or to explain how they feel. Some people like to share a few words at the end, and that's always optional.

What if someone doesn't want to join in?

That's completely fine. Taking part is always a choice. People can follow a lighter version, stay seated, pause whenever they want or simply observe. In my experience, most people who start sceptical end up joining in once they feel safe in the room.

Is it safe for everyone?

The practice is trauma-informed and grounded in Polyvagal Theory, so I never push anyone beyond what their body is ready for. Before the session everyone fills in a short questionnaire that includes a general health check. For a few conditions, like pregnancy or epilepsy, I adapt the practice or ask for medical clearance.

Can it happen in our office?

Yes. Seated formats work well in a meeting room. For practices lying down we need a quiet space with enough floor room for everyone. If you don't have one, I can help you find a venue in your city. After our first call I'll tell you exactly what to prepare.

Does it work online?

Yes. Single sessions and the weekly reset work really well online, so remote and hybrid teams can join from wherever they are. People just need a quiet corner, a chair or a mat, and their camera on if they feel comfortable.

How do we see the impact?

The questionnaire at the start gives us a clear picture of where your team is with stress, sleep and energy. If we work together over several weeks, we repeat it at the end, so you can see the shift in numbers and not only in how people feel.

How far ahead should we book?

Ideally a few weeks before your date, so there is time for the call, the questionnaire and the proposal. Shorter notice is often possible too, so just ask on the first call and we'll find a way.
