What is Breathwork?

Conscious Connected Breathwork is a rhythmic breathing practice done lying down, with music, in a safe and held space. The breath stays continuous, without pauses, which gradually shifts the state of the nervous system and opens access to parts of yourself that are harder to reach in ordinary awareness.

The breath has a way of taking you somewhere the mind alone cannot go. Inner landscapes you didn’t know existed.
A quieter, older part of yourself that has been waiting for space. Sometimes what arises is deep relaxation. Sometimes it is clarity.
Sometimes the nervous system completes a cycle it never had the chance to finish. Sometimes an emotion moves through you that you couldn’t have predicted and wouldn’t know how to explain.

The approach here is not cathartic. The body is treated like an onion, worked with in layers, at its own pace. Each person receives what they need in that moment and no specific outcome is ever the goal. This removes any pressure to feel or do anything in particular, and creates the conditions for something genuine to emerge.

About me

Fabio cancogni

I didn’t find breathwork through curiosity. I found it through necessity: after going through a difficult period with my mental health, the breath became the first thing that actually worked as a direct way back to myself.
That experience changed the direction of everything.

What followed was years of working close to the edges of human experience.
I trained in trauma-informed approaches and worked with medical patients navigating stress, anxiety and nervous system dysregulation.
I brought breathwork into sport, working with high-performance athletes and was part of the support team at the Paris Olympics. Each context taught me something different about what the body holds and what it needs to let go.

Today I work primarily with CEOs, founders and leaders who are high-functioning on the outside and quietly running on empty on the inside. People who have built a lot and somewhere along the way lost the thread back to themselves. The breath, in my experience, is the fastest and most honest way back.

My approach is science-grounded, trauma-aware and deeply intuitive. I listen to what is alive in the room and work with it. Every session is shaped around what the person or group actually needs in that moment.

Safety & Considerations

Trauma-Informed Approach

→ You’re always in control. You can adjust, slow down, or stop at any time.

→ I explain what’s happening so you understand your experience.

→ We work within your window of tolerance, never forcing or pushing.

→ Your body’s wisdom guides the process, not my agenda.

→ Integration and pacing are prioritized over intensity.

Breathwork is generally safe when practiced with proper guidance

However, certain conditions require caution or medical clearance. Safety is always the priority.

Please consult with me before attending if you have:

  • Pregnancy
  • Cardiovascular conditions
  • Epilepsy or seizure history
  • Severe asthma
  • Recent surgery or injury
  • Psychosis or bipolar disorder
Do I need any prior experience?

No. Whether this is your first time in a breathwork or somatic space, or you have done this many times before, you will be met where you are. There is no right or wrong way to show up

What will I actually feel during the practice?


It varies from person to person. Some people feel warmth or tingling moving through the body. Some feel heaviness, then a release into lightness. Some feel emotion rise and pass. Some feel a deep stillness they haven't touched in a long time. The honest answer is that you won't know until you are in it, and that is part of what makes it worth doing.

What if something intense comes up?

The practice is designed in layers. Nothing is forced and the body is always respected. If at any point it feels like too much, you can simply return to normal breathing and the intensity settles quickly. I am present throughout and will notice if you need support before you even have to ask.

Will I be okay to go about my day afterwards?

Most people leave feeling grounded, clear, and quietly open. Some feel a little tender, in a good way, like something has shifted and needs a moment to settle. It is worth keeping the afternoon gentle if you can. A big social event straight after is not ideal, but most people drive home without any issue.

Who usually comes to these sessions?


People at different points in their lives. Some going through a transition, some simply curious, some who have tried everything else and want to try something that works from the inside out. What they tend to have in common is a sense that there is more available to them than what they are currently accessing. You will not be the only first-timer in the room.

Why a group and not a private session?

There is something that happens in a group that cannot be replicated alone. When one person gives themselves permission to go deeper, it creates a kind of permission for everyone else. The shared energy of the room becomes part of the experience. Many people find their first group session more powerful than they expected precisely because of this.
